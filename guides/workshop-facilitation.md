# Workshop Facilitation Guide

## Purpose
This guide covers how we run client discovery sessions, strategy workshops, and review meetings. Workshops are where we build trust, extract insight, and co-create solutions with clients.

---

## 1. Workshop Types

### 1.1 Discovery Session
**Purpose:** Understand the client's current state, pain points, and goals.
**When:** Start of every engagement.
**Duration:** 2-3 hours.
**Participants:** Client stakeholders (5-10 people across relevant functions).

### 1.2 Strategy Workshop
**Purpose:** Co-develop strategic options and evaluate trade-offs with the client team.
**When:** After initial research is complete, before final recommendations.
**Duration:** Half day (3-4 hours).
**Participants:** Client leadership + working team (8-15 people).

### 1.3 Review Meeting
**Purpose:** Present findings or deliverables and get feedback.
**When:** At key milestones and engagement close.
**Duration:** 60-90 minutes.
**Participants:** Client sponsor + relevant stakeholders.

---

## 2. Preparation (1-2 Days Before)

### 2.1 Design the Agenda
Every workshop needs a written agenda sent 24 hours in advance. Include:
- Objective (one sentence: what will be true at the end that isn't true now?)
- Timed agenda blocks (never more than 90 min without a break)
- Pre-read materials (keep under 5 pages; most people won't read more)
- Expected outcomes and decisions

### 2.2 Prepare Materials
- **Facilitation script:** Key questions, transition points, time checks
- **Stimulus materials:** Data, frameworks, examples to spark discussion
- **Capture template:** Pre-formatted document for recording outputs
- **Backup exercises:** If a discussion stalls, have an alternative activity ready

### 2.3 Logistics
- Confirm room, AV setup, and remote access (test video/audio 30 min prior)
- Prepare physical or digital whiteboard
- Have the agenda visible throughout (projected or printed)
- Arrange seating to encourage discussion (U-shape or round table, not theater)

## 3. Facilitation Techniques

### 3.1 Opening (First 15 Minutes)
1. **State the purpose.** One sentence on why we're here and what we'll accomplish.
2. **Set ground rules.** Phones away, one conversation at a time, all perspectives valued.
3. **Check in.** Quick round-robin: name, role, one thing you hope to get from this session.

### 3.2 Core Techniques

#### Silent Brainstorming
**Use when:** Generating ideas without groupthink.
**How:** Each person writes ideas on sticky notes silently for 5 minutes, then shares.
**Why it works:** Prevents the loudest voice from dominating.

#### Dot Voting
**Use when:** Prioritizing options from a long list.
**How:** Each person gets 3-5 dots to place on their top choices.
**Why it works:** Democratic, visual, fast.

#### Forced Ranking
**Use when:** Hard trade-off decisions.
**How:** Stack-rank options against a single criterion. Repeat for each criterion.
**Why it works:** Forces explicit trade-offs instead of "everything is important."

#### "What Would Have to Be True?"
**Use when:** Evaluating strategic options.
**How:** For each option, ask: "What would have to be true for this to be the best choice?"
**Why it works:** Separates option evaluation from option advocacy. Credits: Roger Martin.

#### Pre-Mortem
**Use when:** Stress-testing a plan.
**How:** "Imagine it's one year from now and this initiative has failed. What went wrong?"
**Why it works:** Surfaces risks people are reluctant to raise in a positive planning session.

### 3.3 Managing Dynamics

| Situation | Technique |
|-----------|-----------|
| One person dominates | "Let's hear from someone who hasn't spoken yet." |
| Discussion goes off-topic | "Great point — let's capture that in the parking lot and come back to [topic]." |
| Energy drops | Take a 5-minute break; switch to a physical activity (sticky notes, whiteboard). |
| Disagreement escalates | "It sounds like we have two perspectives. Let's articulate both clearly before evaluating." |
| No one speaks | Use silent brainstorming, then ask people to share what they wrote. |
| Rabbit hole | Time-box: "Let's give this 5 more minutes, then decide if it needs a separate session." |

### 3.4 Closing (Last 15 Minutes)
1. **Summarize outputs.** Read back the key decisions, insights, and open questions.
2. **Confirm next steps.** Who does what by when. Be specific.
3. **Check out.** Quick round-robin: one word for how you're feeling about what we accomplished.

---

## 4. Post-Workshop (Within 48 Hours)

1. **Send notes.** Distribute a structured summary within 24 hours:
   - Decisions made
   - Key insights and themes
   - Open questions and parking lot items
   - Action items with owners and deadlines

2. **Update the engagement plan.** Incorporate workshop outputs into project deliverables.

3. **Internal debrief.** What worked? What would we change? Capture for next time.

---

## 5. Remote Workshop Adaptations

Remote workshops require more structure and shorter blocks.

| In-Person | Remote Adaptation |
|-----------|------------------|
| 3-4 hour block | Break into 2x 90-min sessions |
| Physical sticky notes | Miro, FigJam, or shared doc |
| Round-robin check-in | Chat waterfall (everyone types, hits enter on cue) |
| Whiteboard sketching | Screen share + collaborative doc |
| Reading the room | Explicit check-ins every 30 min: thumbs up/down in chat |
| Side conversations | Breakout rooms for small group exercises |

**Remote-specific rules:**
- Cameras on (strongly encouraged, not forced)
- Use a co-facilitator to monitor chat and manage breakout rooms
- Send materials 48 hours ahead (not 24) since async prep is harder to enforce

## 6. Quality Checklist
- [ ] Agenda sent 24+ hours in advance
- [ ] Facilitation script prepared with time allocations
- [ ] Pre-read materials are concise and relevant
- [ ] AV and tools tested before the session
- [ ] Workshop outputs documented and distributed within 24 hours
- [ ] Parking lot items have owners and follow-up dates
- [ ] Internal debrief completed
