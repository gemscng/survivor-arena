# Research Methodology Guide

## Purpose
This guide defines our standard approach to market research, competitive analysis, and data-driven recommendations. It ensures consistency, rigor, and defensibility across all research engagements.

---

## 1. Research Design

### 1.1 Define the Research Questions
Every research engagement starts by agreeing on 2-4 specific questions with the client. Good research questions are:
- **Specific:** "What is the TAM for AI-powered customer service in North America?" not "How big is the AI market?"
- **Answerable:** Can be addressed with available data and methods within the engagement timeline
- **Actionable:** The answer directly informs a decision the client needs to make

### 1.2 Choose the Methodology
Match the method to the question:

| Question Type | Primary Method | Secondary Method |
|---------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Market sizing | Top-down + bottom-up triangulation | Industry reports, public filings |
| Competitive positioning | Feature/capability mapping | Expert interviews, product trials |
| Customer needs | Stakeholder interviews | Survey data, support ticket analysis |
| Technology evaluation | Hands-on assessment | Vendor briefings, analyst reports |
| Trend identification | Signal scanning | Expert interviews, patent/funding data |

### 1.3 Plan the Research
Before starting, document:
- Questions to answer
- Methods to use
- Data sources to consult
- Sample size / coverage targets
- Timeline and milestones
- Known limitations

## 2. Primary Research

### 2.1 Stakeholder Interviews
**When to use:** Client team discovery, customer insights, expert perspectives.

**Process:**
1. Develop an interview guide (semi-structured, 8-12 questions)
2. Target 5-8 interviews for pattern recognition; 12+ for quantitative claims
3. Record with permission; take structured notes regardless
4. Synthesize within 24 hours while context is fresh

**Interview Guide Structure:**
- Context questions (role, experience, current state)
- Problem questions (pain points, failed attempts, workarounds)
- Impact questions (cost of the problem, urgency)
- Solution questions (ideal outcome, constraints, priorities)

### 2.2 Surveys
**When to use:** Quantitative validation of qualitative findings, preference ranking.

**Standards:**
- Minimum sample size: 30 for directional insights, 100+ for statistical significance
- Keep surveys under 15 questions / 5 minutes
- Pilot with 3-5 respondents before full distribution
- Report response rate and any selection bias

### 2.3 Data Collection
**When to use:** Performance analysis, usage patterns, financial modeling.

**Standards:**
- Document the data source, collection date, and any transformations
- Validate data quality before analysis (completeness, accuracy, recency)
- Use at least two independent sources for critical metrics

## 3. Secondary Research

### 3.1 Source Hierarchy
Prefer sources in this order:
1. **Primary data** we collected (interviews, surveys, hands-on testing)
2. **Public filings** (SEC, annual reports, regulatory filings)
3. **Reputable industry analysts** (Gartner, Forrester, IDC — note publication date)
4. **Academic research** (peer-reviewed, recent)
5. **Trade publications** (TechCrunch, industry blogs — verify independently)
6. **General media** (use only for context, never as sole evidence)

### 3.2 Source Evaluation
For every secondary source, verify:
- **Recency:** Is the data less than 18 months old? If not, flag as potentially stale.
- **Methodology:** Does the source explain how they got their numbers?
- **Bias:** Who funded the research? Does the source have a commercial interest?
- **Corroboration:** Can you find a second source that supports the claim?

### 3.3 Citation Standards
- Cite every factual claim with source and date
- Use footnotes in reports, inline in presentations
- Maintain a source bibliography for every engagement

## 4. Analysis Frameworks

### 4.1 Market Sizing
- **Top-down:** Start from total market, narrow by segments and assumptions
- **Bottom-up:** Start from unit economics, scale by customer count
- **Triangulate:** Both methods should converge within 20%; investigate if they don't
- Always state assumptions explicitly and show sensitivity to key variables

### 4.2 Competitive Analysis
- **Feature matrix:** Capability comparison across key dimensions
- **Positioning map:** Plot competitors on 2 axes most relevant to client's decision
- **SWOT per competitor:** Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats
- Evaluate competitors on what they do, not what they claim (test products when possible)

### 4.3 Trend Analysis
- **Signal scanning:** Track funding rounds, patent filings, job postings, regulatory changes
- **Adoption curves:** Where is the technology/trend on the adoption lifecycle?
- **Impact assessment:** How will this trend affect the client's business specifically?

### 4.4 Data Analysis
- Start with descriptive statistics before advanced analysis
- Visualize data to find patterns before running models
- Report confidence intervals, not just point estimates
- Distinguish correlation from causation in all findings

## 5. Quality Standards

### 5.1 The "So What?" Test
Every finding must pass this test: If a client executive reads this, will they know what to do differently? If not, the finding needs more synthesis.

### 5.2 The Defensibility Test
Could we defend every claim in this report if challenged? Every assertion needs:
- Evidence (data, source, or direct observation)
- Context (why this matters for this specific client)
- Qualification (what we're not saying, caveats)

### 5.3 Peer Review
All research reports go through internal review before delivery:
- Methodology review: Are the methods appropriate for the questions?
- Evidence review: Are claims supported and sources cited?
- Actionability review: Are recommendations specific enough to act on?

---

## 6. Common Pitfalls to Avoid
1. **Confirmation bias:** Seeking data that supports a pre-formed conclusion
2. **Anchoring:** Over-weighting the first data point found
3. **Survivorship bias:** Only studying successful examples
4. **Scope creep:** Researching interesting tangents that don't answer the core questions
5. **Generic findings:** Conclusions that could apply to any company in the industry
6. **Data without insight:** Presenting numbers without explaining what they mean for the client
